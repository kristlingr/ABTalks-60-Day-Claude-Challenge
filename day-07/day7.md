# Day 7: AI Usage Strategy (Task Routing & Model Selection)

## Overview
Day 7 of the ABTalks 60-Day Claude AI Mastery Challenge focuses on **AI Usage Strategy**. The goal is to move beyond basic prompting and learn how to effectively route tasks to the right models based on speed, reasoning requirements, and volume.

## Learning Highlights
- **Model Specialization:** Understanding that not every task requires the most powerful model.
- **Task Routing:** Deciding which AI should handle a specific piece of work based on a formula of Task + Model + Effort.
- **Reasoning Calibration:** Choosing the appropriate level of reasoning effort for a task to optimize outcomes and efficiency.

## Key Frameworks & Concepts

### Framework 1: The Model Tier Strategy
Different models in the Claude family excel at different types of professional work.

**Components:**
- **Claude Haiku (Speed & Volume):** Best for quick summaries, email rewrites, and formatting tasks.
- **Claude Sonnet (The Workhorse):** The standard for research synthesis, strategy documents, and professional coding support.
- **Claude Opus (High-Stakes Reasoning):** Reserved for complex architecture, governance frameworks, and critical decision support.

### Framework 2: The Routing Formula
Effective AI usage is determined by three variables:
- **Right Task:** Identifying the core requirement (e.g., formatting vs. strategy).
- **Right Model:** Selecting the tier that matches the requirement.
- **Right Effort:** Determining if the task needs simple execution or deep reasoning.

## Practical Applications

### Application 1: Operational Efficiency
Using Claude Haiku to automate high-volume, low-complexity tasks like meeting note formatting or routine email drafts to save time and API costs.

### Application 2: Strategic Development
Utilizing Claude Sonnet or Opus for research synthesis and architectural reviews where depth of reasoning and context window are critical for quality.

## Key Takeaways
The most advanced AI users don't just focus on the prompt; they focus on task routing. By choosing the right model and reasoning effort for each specific job, you achieve better, faster, and more cost-effective outcomes.
